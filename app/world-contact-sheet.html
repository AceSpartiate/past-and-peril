PAST & PERIL

The world, at the hours it is actually played at

Every image below is the real renderer reading the real map files — the same code the students' Chromebooks run. Nothing here was drawn for this page. If it looks wrong here, it is wrong in the game.

Seven places. Two towns and five interiors. Every student stands in exactly one, so two who walk into different buildings in the same second are looking at different worlds a beat later. Going inside costs one movement point, not your turn.

The map says what time it is. Each scene declares its hour and weather; the ground is washed for the hour and windows light up after dark. Sunrise and sunset are computed and certain. The clock hours are attested for some events and reconstructed for others, and the game says which. There were no time zones in 1835 — every town kept its own solar time.

Terrain is drawn, not coloured. Grass has blades, fields have furrows running the same way across the whole field, roads are one continuous rutted ribbon, timber casts shadows, high ground gets a surveyor's hachures. Every scattered mark is hashed from its hex coordinate, so thirty screens agree to the pixel and nothing crawls when the canvas redraws.

Run node tools/check-maps.mjs after touching any map. See design/17-the-world.md.
